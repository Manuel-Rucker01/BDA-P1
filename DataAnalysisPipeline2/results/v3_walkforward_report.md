## Setup

* **Universe / eligibility (PIT):** WS1 survivorship-free common stocks (NASDAQ/NYSE/AMEX, active + delisted, reused symbols split into `SYM~k`), eligible on a date when raw close >= $3, 20-session ADV >= $2M and >= 120 bars (`universe.eligible`). Cross-sectional features, z-scores and rank targets use exactly this population (`core.compute_features(..., population=...)` / `core.add_cross_sectional_features`).
* **Features:** the 32 `features/core.py` columns (price/volume only; fundamentals not yet available — WS2 blocked on `SEC_USER_AGENT`), z-scored per date with `core.cross_sectional_zscore` over eligible names. No KG in the candidate recipes.
* **Sectors:** the legacy metadata labels only 1,538 tickers; among eligible rows 26.4% have a real label, 73.2% get a **proxy sector** (known sector with the highest market-residual return correlation over the 120 sessions before the month, recomputed monthly, PIT) and 0.34% stay 'Unknown'. Sector features, sector-neutral targets/IC and every sector cap use this effective sector.
* **Labels:** forward 21/30-session returns on adjusted closes; a stock that stops trading inside the horizon gets the return to its last close (delisting losses count); winsorized to (-0.95, 3.0); targets = per-date percentile ranks over eligible names (raw, or sector-demeaned for `sn30`).
* **Model:** LightGBM + XGBoost + CatBoost regressors, fixed moderate hyperparameters, each early-stopped on the last <=52 weekly dates of its training window (fit part ends 40 sessions before validation), refit on the whole window with the early-stopped tree count, rank-averaged per date.
* **Walk-forward:** weekly decision dates (last session of each week) from 2017; test year Y in 2019..2026 trained on dates t with pos(t) < pos(first session of Y) − 40 (embargo ≥ 30 + 10 sessions). Every 2019–2026 weekly prediction is out of sample.
* **Backtest:** monthly rebalance on the first session of each month using the latest weekly OOS prediction strictly before it, traded at that close; daily mark-to-market; delisted holdings frozen at their last close and sold at the next rebalance; 10 bps per side on traded notional; 1%/yr borrow on shorts; cash at 0%; daily stock returns clipped to (-0.95, 3.0) only where the adjusted and raw returns disagree (corporate-action / adjustment-error days; genuine moves count in full). Period: first rebalance Feb 2019 → 2026-11-04. EW benchmark = all eligible names, monthly, no costs; SPY = Alpaca SIP adjusted.

## Chosen recipe and construction

* Recipe: `base` — the pre-registered rule keeps `base` unless a variant beats its mean IC by >= 0.005 with a higher NW t: `sn30` has +0.0035 IC but a lower t (5.08 vs 5.37); `h21` is lower on both.
* Construction: `legacy_top10`. No construction had max drawdown >= -35% (all long-only books lost 38–40% in the Feb–Mar 2020 crash), so the rule fell back to the highest Sharpe. Among the broad books, `lo30` (Sharpe 0.69, IR vs EW 0.23) is the closest alternative.
* **Artefact:** not produced — the gate failed, so `best_model_v3.pkl` was not built (the builder `python -m DataAnalysisPipeline2.v3.artefact --variant base --construction <c>` is ready and tested). The feature_version-3 live path in `trading_agent/bot.py` is implemented and tested end-to-end on synthetic data (`trading_agent/test_feature_v3_inference.py`: live features, z-scored inputs and predictions equal the offline chunked dataset) but stays dormant until a v3 artefact is promoted.

## Caveats

* **No fundamentals yet.** WS2 (SEC EDGAR) is blocked on `SEC_USER_AGENT`; the recipe is price/volume only. Fundamentals plug in as an extra feature block in `v3/dataset.py` stage B (merge on ticker/Date by filing date) and would need a new walk-forward + gate run.
* **Sectors are mostly proxies** (see Setup). Sector caps and sector-neutral books rely on return-correlation sectors for ~70% of eligible names; EDGAR SIC codes should replace them.
* **Delisting returns** use the last exchange close; post-delisting OTC losses (e.g. bankruptcies) are not captured, so short books are flattered slightly and long books penalised slightly less than reality.
* **Shorting**: no historical easy-to-borrow data — every eligible name is assumed shortable at a flat 1%/yr; hard-to-borrow small caps can cost far more.
* **Execution**: trades at the rebalance close at 10 bps; no market impact model (books hold 30–80 names of eligible stocks with >= $2M ADV).
* **Price data**: Alpaca SIP adjusted bars; ~62 residual adjustment-error jumps remain in the store — labels are winsorized; backtest returns are clipped only on days where adjusted and raw returns disagree.
* **Post-hoc engine fix (disclosed).** The first evaluation pass clipped *every* daily return at +300%. Inspecting the long/short book showed this hid genuine short squeezes (QMMM +1,737% on 2025-09-09, raw and adjusted agreeing) — a bias in favour of shorts. The clip was restricted to adjustment-disagreement days (test `test_clip_only_adjustment_disagreements`) and everything was re-run. Gate thresholds and selection rules were not touched; the change only lowered the L/S results (base L/S cum. return 2,582% → 1,315%), long-only books are unchanged.
* **The long/short book is not investable as simulated.** Weights are reset only monthly, so a short that squeezes 17x grows to >50% of NAV intra-month: the book loses 94% on 2025-09-09 (QMMM) and its vol (~300%), Sharpe and drawdown (−96%) are dominated by a handful of meme squeezes. The short leg is the most volatile, lowest-ranked micro-caps — exactly the names with prohibitive borrow fees and recalls. A real L/S implementation needs per-name stop/resize rules, borrow data (ETB/fee) and a volatility filter on the short leg; its numbers here should not be read as an achievable return.
* **Universe residue**: the WS1 universe still contains (i) SPAC shells and pending-merger targets (near-zero volatility, ~$10 trust value) that inverse-vol weighting favours, and (ii) at least one duplicate security under two symbols whose adjusted closes differ by a constant factor (ATNF/FRMM, x10), which `detect_duplicates` misses because it matches on adjusted closes. Both should be fixed in WS1 (flag SPACs from SEC SIC 6770 once EDGAR is available; match duplicates on raw close + volume).
* **2021 fold early-stopped at the 50-tree floor** for all three models (its validation window was the 2020 crash/rebound); 2020 is the one negative-IC year (momentum/low-vol crash).
* **Benchmark EW** is gross of costs (a conservative bar for gate b).
* **2026** is a partial year (labels end 30 sessions before the last bar, so the last ~6 weekly dates have no IC; the backtest runs to the last bar).
* **Trials**: DSR counts the configurations evaluated in this run; earlier project iterations (v1/v2) are not re-counted because the v3 recipe and gate were fixed in advance.

### What changed

* **Sectors:** EDGAR SIC → Fama-French 12 (`sectors.parquet`, `sector_name`) per ticker id, resolved through the SEC ticker→CIK map at the ticker's last bar (segment ids `SYM~k` via their base symbol and the map's validity windows). Names without an EDGAR sector get the round-1 return-correlation proxy, now referenced to the EDGAR sectors. Used everywhere sector is used: `sector_daily_return`/`sector_return_5d`, the sector-neutral target and IC, and every per-sector cap in the books. Caveat: SIC codes are the current EDGAR snapshot (not point-in-time); reclassifications are rare, but this part is not strictly PIT.
* **Fundamentals** (`FundamentalsBlock`, 14 columns: E/P, B/P, S/P, CF/P, GP/A, ROA, ROE, accruals, sales growth, scaled NI growth, leverage, 12-month net issuance, log market cap, filing staleness): `fundamentals.fundamental_features`, an as-of join on `asof_date` = next business day after the EDGAR **filing** date (never the period end); market cap = raw close × shares as filed. Z-scored per date over eligible names like every other feature.
* **Survivorship leak found and neutralised.** The ticker→CIK map is built mostly from the SEC's *current* ticker list: 98.7% of eligible rows of names still trading in Oct-2026 are mapped vs 21.5% of names that later delisted, and mapped rows have a higher mean future rank (≈0.51 vs ≈0.48 in every year 2017–2024). Fed naively, "fundamentals missing" would tell the model the stock is going to die. Fix: every eligible row without an EDGAR record receives the complete fundamentals vector of a same-date donor drawn (deterministic hash) among its 5 nearest mapped neighbours on liquidity, volatility, 20/50-day momentum and price level. `fund_imputed` is kept for diagnostics, never as a feature.
* **Leak probe** (LightGBM classifier predicting `fund_imputed` from model inputs, 2017–2022, held-out dates): AUC core only 0.675 (legitimate — future delisters trade differently), fundamentals only 0.647, core+fund 0.700. The fundamentals add +0.025 AUC of separability (a naive liquidity-decile hot-deck added +0.110). A small residual channel remains and is a caveat on the fundamentals' measured value.

### Recommendation

**Do not promote v3 yet.** The pre-registered gate fails on one criterion — Deflated Sharpe
(b3) — and that is the one that matters most: with 32 configurations tried, the best long-only book's
Sharpe (~0.56) is indistinguishable from the expected maximum of 32 noise trials (SR* ≈ 0.59). The
chosen book `lo50` beats the EW universe on Sharpe only through lower volatility (IR vs EW ≈ −0.07,
bootstrap CI of Sharpe − EW Sharpe spans 0), it underperforms SPY, and its drawdown passes b2 by
one point (−34.0% vs −35%).

What round 2 did show:
* **The signal is real and stable** (weekly IC 0.065, NW t 4.8, positive in 7/8 years; holdout IC
  0.143 for `sn30_fund`, above v2's 0.14 reference) — the problem is converting it into a long-only
  book that beats cheap beta, not the ranking itself.
* **EDGAR sectors** (`base` → `base_sec`) did not raise IC (0.067 → 0.064); their value is correct
  risk buckets for caps and sector-neutral targets.
* **Fundamentals** (`base_sec` → `base_fund`) did not raise walk-forward IC either (0.064 → 0.060)
  but lifted the recent holdout (0.112 → 0.132) and cut long-only drawdowns (−39% → −35%); with the
  sector-neutral target (`sn30_fund`) IC returns to 0.065 and holdout to 0.143. Treat the holdout gain
  cautiously: it is 50 days of one regime, and a small residual imputation channel remains.
* **COVID**: excluding Feb–Apr 2020, long-only drawdowns fall to −27…−33% (SPY −24.5%), so most of
  the b2 margin is the crash; the 2022 bear (−24%) is the next-worst episode.
* The long/short book remains uninvestable as simulated (squeeze-driven ruin).

Suggested next steps (each would be a new, pre-registered round with N counted): a broad long-only
book with a market-cap/liquidity floor (to stop the book tilting into micro-caps that EW and SPY
don't hold), a beta/vol-targeted overlay, and an L/S version with a volatility filter and borrow
data on the short leg. Until one clears DSR, keep v2final in production.

them (`sn30_all`, ensemble or LGBM-only) gives +0.001. The trees already extract what these blocks add from
the 32 price/volume features + the 14 fundamentals: e.g. the explicit low-vol/quality composite raises IC
slightly but *lowers* book Sharpe (0.46 vs 0.58), i.e. the model already holds that tilt. Residual momentum
gives the best recent holdout (0.147) but the worst walk-forward IC (0.062) — not a robust gain.

**Which model should trade (initial paper buy): keep the current production `v2final_nonews`; do not
deploy a v3 artefact.** Evidence:
* Pre-registered rule: v3 replaces v2 only if it passes the gate; after four rounds (55 trials) it has not.
* Head-to-head on the only window where both have genuine out-of-sample scores (feature dates

* v3's 8-year walk-forward proves the *ranking* is durable (IC > 0 in 7/8 years), but no long-only book built
  from it beat SPY or the EW universe on IR over 2019–2026 — there is no evidence that switching would
  improve the paper account's returns.
* Caveat on v2: `v2final_nonews` has no multi-year out-of-sample record (trained 2024-10 → 2026-09, one
  regime), the round-1 diagnosis that such models decay within ~9 months still applies — keep WS5's monthly

The decisive missing experiment is a 2019–2026 walk-forward of the *v2 recipe* itself on this
survivorship-free store (same folds/books): only that can say whether v2's recent edge is a regime artefact.
It needs the v2 training pipeline, which is not in this branch.

## Round 5 — risk model & optimizer

This round changes portfolio construction only. It adds a point-in-time factor risk model, a per-name transaction-cost model, and a cost-aware mean-variance optimizer, all evaluated on the incumbent `sn30_fund` walk-forward OOS predictions (no retraining). The configs, λ/TE/cost settings, alpha scaling, selection rule, trial count (N = 55 + 16 = 71) and a new primary criterion (IR vs SPY > 0, with a bootstrap 95% CI) were committed to `v3/gate.py` (`R5_*`) in commit `af19d25`, before any round-5 backtest or bias statistic existed. The runner was committed in `5033717`, also before any result. Code: `v3/risk.py`, `v3/costs.py`, `v3/optimizer.py`, `v3/round5.py`. Tests: `v3/tests/test_{risk,costs,optimizer,round5}.py`.

### What was built

* **Risk model (`risk.py`)**, estimated on every decision date from data up to that close:
  * Factors: 8 styles (beta 252d, size = log cleaned mcap, value = B/P+E/P, momentum 12-1, reversal 1m, volatility 60d ⊥ beta/size, liquidity = log ADV/mcap ⊥ size, quality = GP/A+ROA) plus 12 EDGAR FF12 industries. Styles are winsorised, have cap-weighted mean 0 and EW sd 1.
  * Factor returns: daily WLS with √mcap weights.
  * Factor covariance: EWMA (half-life 90) with Newey-West (2 lags).
  * Specific variance: EWMA (half-life 90), with USE4 Bayesian shrinkage to the sector cap-weighted mean.
  * USE4 volatility-regime adjustment.
  * Market cap: EDGAR close × shares filed. Names without their own EDGAR record, foreign private issuers (20-F/40-F: share count ≠ ADS count) and >20× outliers instead get a same-date ADV/price fit. Examples of bad raw EDGAR values: HXL at $5·10¹⁵, TSM at 5× its true value.
  * Speed: the full 2016–2026 panel (8.4k ids × 2.7k sessions) fits in ~80 s.
  * Point-in-time behaviour is tested: changing future returns leaves the model unchanged.
* **Cost model (`costs.py`)**:
  * Half-spread: 0.5 × the 63-day mean Corwin-Schultz estimate. The price/ADV proxy is used when fewer than 20 estimates exist.
  * Impact: k·σ₆₀·√(trade/ADV₂₀) with k = 0.2.
  * Borrow: 0.25% / 1% / 5% a year by market cap.
  * In the optimizer this is exactly c|Δw| + d|Δw|^{3/2} with d = kσ√(AUM/ADV).
  * The backtest prices every trade with the same model, at NAV-scaled AUM.
* **Optimizer (`optimizer.py`)**:
  * Algorithm: ADMM in the OSQP form. Box, linear (budget/sector/beta/style), cost-prox, TE-ball and short-budget blocks each have a closed-form projection or prox. The x-update uses a Woodbury solve on diag + rank-(K+m), so no N×N matrix is ever formed.
  * Accuracy: matches SLSQP on random problems to 2·10⁻⁷ in objective.
  * Speed: a 2,865-name problem solves in 0.6–1.8 s. All ~2,100 rebalance solves in the backtests converged; none hit the iteration cap.
  * Alpha: α = IC_t·σ_spec·z (Grinold). IC_t is the trailing mature weekly IC shrunk to a 0.03 prior (26 pseudo-weeks).
  * The live entry point is `optimize_portfolio`. It takes any monotone score, so v2 `pred_rank` works as well as v3 `pred`.

### Recommendation (round 5)

**Do not promote; keep `v2final_nonews` in production. `optimized_v3` was not wired into `portfolio.py`/`bot.py`,** because no config passed the gate. The infrastructure is ready if a future signal clears it:
* `optimizer.optimize_portfolio` accepts v2 `pred_rank` or v3 `pred`.
* `risk.live_panel` builds the risk-model panel from `market_data.fetch_daily_bars` output plus the EDGAR files. Not yet covered by a test.

What round 5 established:
1. The construction problem is solved to an institutional standard. The risk model is calibrated (bias ≈1 outside crashes), costs are explicit, and the optimizer is fast and exact. Its best book (absolute MV) reaches Sharpe 0.97 and DSR 0.86, the closest any v3 book has come to the bar.
2. What stops the system beating SPY is the **signal**, not the book. Its skill is confined to small and mid caps (top-500 IC 0.002, t 0.2). Those are exactly the names a cap-weighted benchmark and a TE budget barely touch, and where trading costs are highest. No benchmark-relative large-cap book can add value with it.
3. Two methodological flaws were found after the fact. They are disclosed and were **not** used to re-run any config:
   * The trailing IC should be estimated **per universe**. A top-500 IC of ~0 would have set alpha ≈ 0 and the TE books would have hugged the benchmark.
   * The Corwin-Schultz half-spread is biased upward for large caps on daily bars: median 24–34 bp in the top-500 vs ~1.5 bp from the price/ADV proxy. Costs are therefore conservative by roughly 0.5–1%/yr. This does not change the verdict: the TE books' gross active returns, before any cost, are negative.

Next steps (each would be a new pre-registered round with N counted):
* A large-cap-specific model, trained and evaluated on the top-1000.
* A small/mid-cap mandate benchmarked to a CW small/mid-cap index, where the IC lives.
* Per-universe IC and an eigenfactor risk adjustment in the optimizer.
* Quoted-spread data instead of Corwin-Schultz for large caps.

### Findings

* **The v2 recipe's ranking edge does not survive the optimizer.** Scored on tradable names it has the highest IC (0.0725 vs 0.0653), but through `mv_abs_all` it is *worse* than the incumbent signal (Sharpe 0.87 vs 0.97, IR vs SPY −0.30 vs −0.17): the optimizer's alpha is the IC-scaled score times specific vol, and v2's extra skill sits in the volatile small names the risk/cost terms down-weight.
* **Stacking + `mv_abs_all` is the best book of the project** (Sharpe 0.98, max DD −29.8%, IR vs EW +0.22) — essentially tied with Round 5's incumbent (0.97); DSR 0.862 is just below 0.90 and IR vs SPY is still negative (−0.16, CI −0.82…0.50). It beats SPY on risk (vol 15% vs 19%, DD −30% vs −34%) but not on return (14.7% vs 16.1% CAGR).
* **Eigenfactor adjustment does not fix the risk bias** (realized/ex-ante vol 1.19 → 1.17) and slightly lowers performance: the under-prediction is not in the factor covariance's eigen-spectrum but in specific risk / concentration of the optimized book.
* **K=30 heuristic books** remain clearly worse than the optimizer (Sharpe 0.52–0.64, DD −35…−38%).

### Recommendation (round 7)

**Nothing should replace production `v2final_nonews` + `long_only_v3` K=30 on the evidence of the
pre-registered gate.** Every v3 book still fails IR vs SPY > 0 (best −0.16) and the DSR bar (best 0.862 at
N = 85) — i.e. none has shown that it beats simply holding SPY after costs.

Two caveats for the user's decision (not gate outcomes):
* The production K=30 heuristic book is itself the weakest construction in every round (Sharpe 0.5–0.65,
  max DD −35…−39% in the 2019–2026 walk-forwards, for both v2- and v3-style rankings). If a change were made
  on risk grounds, the evidence favours the optimizer (`mv_abs_all`: Sharpe ~0.97, max DD −30%, vol 15%)
  over K=30 — but it would be a risk-reduction choice, not an alpha one, and it fails the pre-registered
  gate, so it should go to the paper account only as an explicit, user-approved experiment.
* If the goal is to beat SPY, the honest finding of seven rounds is that this signal family (price/volume +
  EDGAR fundamentals, IC ≈ 0.07, ~no large-cap skill) does not do it after costs. Next steps would be new
  information (analyst revisions via the ready adapter, alternative data) rather than further model or
  construction search, which would keep raising N.
